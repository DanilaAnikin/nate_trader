### 2. Vol-targeted position sizing — REVERTED

Reason: the highest-alpha names in this strategy (SMCI, MSTR, APP)

2. A regime where leveraged sleeves intentionally underdeploy.

## Why two features failed

The strategy's design is fundamentally "deploy 100% of capital in
leveraged ETFs (TQQQ + UPRO) when the SMA gate is on; sit in cash
otherwise." This leaves no room for parallel sleeves to add alpha:

- **SGOV** wants to grab idle cash at the T-bill rate, but the
  leveraged sleeves want that same cash at 3× SPY/QQQ exposure.
  Even when SGOV is sized to only the residual headroom, regime
  transitions cause buy/sell churn that costs more than the yield gain.

- **PEAD** wants to buy gap-up names, but the cash for them sits in
  TQQQ. PEAD can only act when the SMA gate is OFF — and during
  gate-off periods, gap-ups in choppy markets often reverse.

**Vol-targeting** failed for a different reason: the high-vol names
ARE the strategy's biggest winners. Shrinking them via vol-targeting
trades alpha for Sharpe.

## What did work

**UPRO** worked because it operates inside the leveraged-sleeve allocation
itself, not in parallel to it. 100% → 75/25 TQQQ/UPRO is a substitution,
not an addition. The total leverage exposure is unchanged; only the
underlying-index diversification improves.

## Reflection

What worked: tight one-knob-at-a-time iteration with `experiment.py`.
30+ targeted backtests in ~3 hours, each isolating one variable.
The +12.7pp WF lift came almost entirely from one change (UPRO
addition); the other three features I tried regressed and I
correctly reverted them rather than ship.

What didn't: my initial hypothesis that all 4 features would compound
was wrong. The leveraged-sleeves-dominate-cash structure of this
strategy means parallel sleeves (PEAD, SGOV) can't earn alpha when
TQQQ/UPRO are at full deployment. Vol-targeting was wrong because
the alpha source IS high-vol names. Lesson: in a strategy this
concentrated, look for substitutions inside the dominant sleeve,
not additions outside it.
